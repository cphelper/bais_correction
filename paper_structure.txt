Introduction

Context:
- Ocean wave climate projections (Significant Wave Height, SWH) are needed for coastal risk assessment and maritime infrastructure planning.
- The Indian Ocean wave climate is highly non-stationary due to monsoons (SW monsoon in JJA, NE monsoon in DJF) and Southern Ocean swell influence.

Study Region:
- Indian Ocean domain: 50°–110°E, 70°S–70°N.
- Common analysis grid: 2° (monthly means).

Research Problem ending with Objectives

Research Problem:
- CMIP6-based SWH products (or CMIP6-forced wave chains) show systematic, spatially structured biases versus satellite altimeter observations.
- Traditional univariate bias correction (e.g., Quantile Mapping) can match distributions but often ignores spatial/seasonal structure and assumes stationarity of bias.

Objectives:
- Quantify uncertainty in raw CMIP6 SWH over the Indian Ocean against satellite altimetry (bias, RMSE, correlation).
- Build a satellite-guided AI/ML bias-correction model for SWH that captures non-linear and state-dependent bias patterns.
- Validate correction on unseen years (proxy for future application).
- Provide a transferable workflow that can be applied to future CMIP6 SSP scenarios for bias-corrected projections.

Literature Review

Traditional Statistical Bias Correction:
- Quantile Mapping (QM): maps model CDF to observed CDF; strong for distribution matching (including tails when data-rich).
- Limitations: univariate and grid-point-wise; may break spatial coherence/physical consistency; assumes stationary bias transfer; tail sampling limitations for rare extremes.

Machine Learning Methods:
- Random Forest (RF):
  - Lightweight and CPU-friendly; learns non-linear mappings using tabular features.
  - Requires explicit spatial/temporal covariates (lat/lon/month) to capture regional and seasonal bias.
  - Strong interpretability (feature importance) but no built-in physical constraints.
- Deep Learning (CNN/U-Net):
  - Captures spatial coherence and multi-scale patterns; can use multichannel inputs.
  - High compute cost, data-hungry; risk of poor generalization under distribution shift (“gray swan” rare events).

Hybrid / Physics-Informed Directions (motivation for future work):
- Hybrid QM + RF/ML: use QM for tail distribution alignment and ML for spatiotemporal structure.
- Physics-informed ML (PIML/PINNs): embed physical constraints (e.g., conservation laws) to improve robustness and physical consistency.

Methodology

Datasets:
- Model (CMIP6 proxy): `cowcliphs.nc`
  - Monthly SWH, ~0.5° grid.
  - Initial grid points: 151,200; valid ocean samples after masking/NaNs: 85,796.
- Observations (satellite altimetry): `io-altimeter.nc`
  - Daily mean SWH, 2.0° grid; used as reference “truth”.
- Period analyzed: Jan 2015 – Dec 2020.

Preprocessing:
- Harmonize coordinate names and units.
- Align time period and compute monthly means.
- Regrid CMIP6 proxy to the 2.0° observational grid (bilinear interpolation).

Features and Targets (tabular learning):
- Features: `model_swh`, `lat`, `lon`, `month` (1–12).
- Target: `obs_swh` (altimeter SWH).

Train/Test Protocol (temporal split):
- Train: 2015–2018 (57,228 samples).
- Test: 2019–2020 (28,568 samples), simulating “future/unseen”.

Model:
- RandomForestRegressor (n_estimators=50, max_depth=10, random_state=42).
- Output: bias-corrected SWH prediction consistent with satellite reference during overlap period.
- Persist model artifact: `swh_correction_model.pkl`.

Evaluation Metrics:
- Bias (mean error), RMSE, temporal correlation, and \(R^2\) for corrected predictions on test period.

Work Done and Results

Raw (Uncorrected) CMIP6 Proxy vs Satellite (2015–2020):
- Mean Bias: +0.1349 m (systematic overestimation).
- Mean RMSE: 0.5142 m.
- Mean temporal correlation: 0.6916.
- Strong spatial heterogeneity; notable biases reported in:
  - Arabian Sea: +0.24 m
  - Bay of Bengal: +0.18 m
  - Southern Ocean: +0.31 m

AI/ML Correction Performance (Test: 2019–2020):
- RMSE: 0.6103 m → 0.4080 m (33.2% reduction).
- Bias: +0.1788 m → −0.0130 m (~93% reduction).
- \(R^2\) (corrected): 0.8798.

Interpretation / Key Takeaways:
- RF learns spatial + seasonal bias patterns using lat/lon/month.
- Correction tightens raw-vs-obs scatter around 1:1 line and reduces systematic offset.
- Diagnostic plots available in `plots/` (e.g., bias maps, time series, distribution diagnostics, ML diagnostics).

Work Proposed During the Next Year

Phase 2 / Extensions:
- Extend validation to longer altimeter records (e.g., 1992–2024 where available) to assess decadal variability and robustness.
- Improve tail/extremes handling:
  - Hybrid QM + RF approach (QM for tails, RF for mean/spatiotemporal structure).
  - Regime-based models (separate models by SWH bins or season) for rare-event performance.

Model and Feature Enhancements:
- Add physically relevant covariates (if available): wind speed, pressure, SST, climate indices (ENSO/IOD) to improve non-stationary generalization.
- Multi-model correction: calibrate per CMIP6 model/member and develop ensemble weighting based on regional skill.

Broader Scope:
- Extend framework to Sea Level Anomaly (SLA) correction with uncertainty-aware models (e.g., QRNN/DeepAR) where appropriate.
- Explore physics-informed constraints (PIML/PINNs) for better physical consistency and out-of-distribution stability.

Deployment:

Conclusion

Summary:
- CMIP6 proxy SWH over the Indian Ocean exhibits systematic positive bias and spatially structured errors relative to satellite altimetry.
- A satellite-guided Random Forest bias correction using simple covariates (SWH, lat, lon, month) substantially improves accuracy on unseen years.
- The workflow is computationally efficient, reproducible, and transferable to future scenario applications.

Key Deliverables:
- Trained model: `swh_correction_model.pkl`.
- Reproducible scripts and plots in `plots/` supporting diagnostics and evaluation.
